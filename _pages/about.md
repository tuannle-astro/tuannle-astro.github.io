I am an M.Sc. student in theoretical physics at the University of Science, Vietnam National University Ho Chi Minh City (VNUHCM–US). 

I work with the [Galaxy Dynamics Lab](https://tghuybu.github.io/nddteam/index.html) at the University of Michigan, led by [Dr. Dieu D. Nguyen](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=Koy8FSEAAAAJ&hl=en). The group studies how central black holes form and grow together with their host galaxies, using observations from JWST, HST, ALMA, VLT, Keck and simulations for the upcoming ELT.

My part is the measurement itself: I map the motions of stars and molecular gas in the vicinity of galaxy centres with integral-field spectroscopy and interferometry, and use them to weigh the central black holes, from supermassive black holes in nearby galaxies to, more recently, intermediate-mass black holes.

I am applying to PhD programs starting in Fall 2027.

## Research in brief

Since black holes emit no light, we normally detect them in three ways (to put it playfully): we look at them (the accretion disk imaged by the Event Horizon Telescope), we listen to them (gravitational waves detected by LIGO), and we feel them (their gravitational effect on surrounding matter). *My research focuses on this third way !!!*

In the vicinity of a black hole, there is a small region where its gravity is stronger than everything else around it. We call this its sphere of influence, or SOI. Stars and gas in this region are affectted by the black hole, so they move in distinctive orbits. By accurately measuring these movements with advanced technologies, we can estimate the black hole's mass and explore how it shapes the formation and evolution of its host galaxy.

Some examples:

- **Intermediate-mass black holes** with Keck/OSIRIS: a first dynamical measurement of the black hole in IC 342, the "Hidden Galaxy" obscured behind the dusty plane of the Milky Way (submitted to ApJ).
- **Stellar dynamics** with JWST/NIRSpec, e.g. the first robust stellar-dynamical mass of the black hole in the nearby spiral galaxy M81 ([ApJ 2026](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ae64f0)).
- **Molecular-gas dynamics** with ALMA CO observations, e.g. a billion-solar-mass black hole in NGC 315 ([ApJ 2026](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/aea151)) and the first dynamical black hole mass in the radio galaxy NGC 3862 (submitted to ApJ)
- **Mock observations** for the Extremely Large Telescope (HARMONI, MICADO), showing that black hole masses can be measured to ~10 per cent accuracy out to z ≈ 2, far beyond the local Universe ([MNRAS 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stag238)).

## How I got here

I transitioned into astrophysics from a mechanical engineering background, with astronomy as a parallel passion through a student club. In 2022, while still an engineering undergraduate, I met my advisor and gradually shifted from amateur astronomy to professional research on supermassive black hole mass measurement. I later returned to research full-time as a Research Assistant in the [Simons Astrophysics Group at ICISE](https://ifirse.icise.vn/sagi-group.html) in Quy Nhon, Vietnam, and subsequently started an M.Sc. in Theoretical Physics to build the solid physics foundation needed for a long-term research career. Next, I hope to continue this work in a PhD program.
